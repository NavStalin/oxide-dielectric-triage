# Reading list (talk and design-note background)

All entries were confirmed to exist on 2026-11-18 (title, venue, year, DOI). The one-line takeaways come
from abstracts and summaries, not full reads. Read the paper before quoting it in the talk.

## Why evidence first, and why not an LLM for everything

1. **Walters & Wilder (2023), "Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT."**
   *Scientific Reports* 13, 14045. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5
   55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated. This is the case for "code produces citations".
2. **Bran et al. (2024), "Augmenting large language models with chemistry tools" (ChemCrow).**
   *Nature Machine Intelligence* 6, 525–535. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00832-8
   LLMs struggle with chemistry on their own; expert tools do the domain work. Same split as this project.
3. **Boiko et al. (2023), "Autonomous chemical research with large language models" (Coscientist).**
   *Nature* 624, 570–578. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06792-0
   The LLM plans; documentation, code and instruments do the work. Shows where an agent loop fits once lab access exists.

## Why check claims against known answers (the closest analogue to the `ehull` catch)

4. **Bartel et al. (2020), "A critical examination of compound stability predictions from machine-learned formation energies."**
   *npj Computational Materials* 6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00362-y
   Accurate formation energies don't guarantee accurate stability. Stability is relative to competing phases, which is why we recompute the hull.
5. **Cheetham & Seshadri (2024), "Artificial intelligence driving materials discovery? Perspective on the article: Scaling deep learning for materials discovery."**
   *Chemistry of Materials* 36, 3490–3495. https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemmater.4c00643
   Experimental chemists find little evidence that the predicted "new" materials are novel, credible and useful. A case for domain checks.
6. **Leeman et al. (2024), "Challenges in high-throughput inorganic materials prediction and autonomous synthesis."**
   *PRX Energy* 3, 011002. https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXEnergy.3.011002
   Argues the A-Lab's claimed new materials were not verified as new. Automation without expert checks overclaims.

## The science behind the scoring

7. **Sun et al. (2016), "The thermodynamic scale of inorganic crystalline metastability."**
   *Science Advances* 2, e1600225. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.1600225
   How far above the hull real, made materials sit. Background for the 0–100 meV/atom stability scale.
8. **Tran & Blaha (2009), "Accurate band gaps of semiconductors and insulators with a semilocal exchange-correlation potential."**
   *Physical Review Letters* 102, 226401. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.102.226401
   The TBmBJ method. Why it is preferred over the standard functional for gaps.
9. **Robertson (vol. 69, 2006; some indexes list 2005), "High dielectric constant gate oxides for metal oxide Si transistors."**
   *Reports on Progress in Physics* 69, 327–396. https://doi.org/10.1088/0034-4885/69/2/R02
   The standard high-κ review: the κ vs gap trade-off, band offsets, interfaces. Why HfO2 won.
10. **Choudhary et al. (2020), "The joint automated repository for various integrated simulations (JARVIS) for data-driven materials design."**
    *npj Computational Materials* 6, 173. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00440-1
    The primary data source. Cite it in the README and design note.

Suggested order if short on time: 1, 4, 6, 2, then 9 for the science.
